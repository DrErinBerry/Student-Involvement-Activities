📚 Literature & Self-Determination Connection

Reflecting on How to Integrate Skills into Your Existing Curriculum

Activity Instructions

Think about the books, short stories, or other literature you already use in your classroom or with your students. Now that you've learned about the four key self-determination skills—self-advocacy, choice-making, conflict resolution, and persistence—it's time to connect them to your existing curriculum.

Your task: Identify one piece of literature you currently use and explain how you could incorporate self-determination skills into discussions or activities around that text.

💡 Example

Book/Story: Wonder by R.J. Palacio (6th-8th grade)

Self-Determination Skill: Self-advocacy

How I'll incorporate it: After reading the section where Auggie decides to attend school, I'll facilitate a class discussion about his decision-making process. Students will write about a time they had to advocate for themselves or make a difficult choice about something important to them. We'll connect Auggie's courage to advocate for his needs to their own experiences with self-advocacy.

Questions you'll reflect on:

  1. What grade level(s) do you work with?
  2. What book, short story, or text do you currently use?
  3. How do you currently use this text in your classroom?
  4. Which self-determination skill(s) could you emphasize? (self-advocacy, choice-making, conflict resolution, persistence)
  5. How will you incorporate self-determination skills into this text?
  6. When do you plan to implement this?
  7. What materials or preparation will you need?
  8. Why did you choose this particular text and skill combination?

💡 How to Use Your Reflection

This reflection is designed to help you plan concrete ways to integrate self-determination skills into your existing curriculum. Use your completed reflection to:

  • Guide your lesson planning for the upcoming weeks
  • Share with colleagues who teach similar content
  • Track your implementation progress over the semester
  • Reference during IEP meetings when discussing student skill development
