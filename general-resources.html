Top 10 Must-Have Resources

Ten high-value resources to explore beyond the course — starting with Oklahoma’s own statewide transition hub. These are the resources we most recommend you bookmark and return to.

  • 1. Plan Your Way OK

    Oklahoma’s central hub for transition to adulthood, funded by the Oklahoma Pathways to Partnerships (OP2P) project. Organized by role — students, families, professionals, and businesses — it gathers resources, events, training, and partner connections for transition planning, self-determination, and student involvement statewide. Your first stop for Oklahoma-specific support.

  • 2. Oklahoma Secondary Transition Framework & Outcomes

    The state’s comprehensive roadmap for secondary transition, with identified student Outcomes organized by category and grade band — including a full Self-Determination strand (choice-making, goal-setting, self-advocacy, and more) from early elementary through post-secondary. An invaluable reference for writing meaningful, developmentally appropriate goals.

  • 3. Self-Determination Curriculum (PYWOK)

    A comprehensive self-determination curriculum for children and youth ages 5 to 22, available through Plan Your Way OK. Provides ready-to-use, age-spanning instruction in the skills at the heart of student involvement.

  • 4. Self-Directed IEP Teacher’s Manual (OU Zarrow)

    The teacher’s manual for the Self-Directed IEP, part of the ChoiceMaker curriculum from the University of Oklahoma Zarrow Institute. A step-by-step guide for teaching students to lead their own IEP meetings — one of the most evidence-based practices in the field.

  • 5. OU National Center for Disability Education and Training (NCDET)

    A University of Oklahoma center that designs and delivers competency-based training to improve independent living, employment, and career opportunities for people with disabilities. A strong source of professional learning and training tools.

  • 6. OU Pre-ETS (Pre-Employment Transition Services)

    Free Pre-Employment Transition Services for Oklahoma students with disabilities, funded by DRS and run by OU NCDET. Provides job exploration, work-based learning, workplace readiness, post-secondary counseling, and self-advocacy instruction delivered directly in schools.

  • 7. Oklahoma Department of Rehabilitation Services (DRS)

    The state agency that helps Oklahomans with disabilities prepare for, find, and keep employment and live more independently. A key transition partner to invite to IEP meetings and to connect students with as they plan for life after high school.

  • 8. Developmental Disabilities Council of Oklahoma (DDCO)

    A state council that promotes advocacy, leadership, and systems change for Oklahomans with developmental disabilities and their families. Sponsors the Oklahoma Youth Leadership Forum (YLF) and other leadership and self-advocacy opportunities for students.

  • 9. Oklahoma Commission on Children and Youth (OCCY)

    A state commission that coordinates and monitors services for Oklahoma children and youth, fostering joint planning across public and private agencies. A useful systems-level connection for understanding the broader network of supports available to students and families.

  • 10. Celebration Certificates

    Free, printable certificate templates to recognize student milestones and contributions during the IEP process. 123 Certificates offers more grown-up designs suited to secondary and transition-age students; Education World has options better suited to younger students. Small acts of recognition make a significant difference in student motivation.

Your Course Toolkit

The key templates and tools from the training modules, gathered here so they are easy to find and reuse. You have already worked with these throughout the course.

  • Bell Ringer Collection

    Choose one set matched to your students’ level: Intro to Transition, Early Transition, Me!, or Fundamental. Quick daily activities that build self-determination skills consistently over time.

  • One-Pager Template

    A student-created snapshot of strengths, preferences, and goals — ideal for sharing with IEP teams and new teachers. Available in high school and intellectual disability versions.

  • IEP Script (grade-appropriate)

    A customizable speaking guide that helps students prepare what to say at their own IEP meetings. Available in multiple grade-level versions so every student has a voice-appropriate entry point.

  • Me! Scale or AIR Self-Determination Scale

    Free, research-based assessment tools that measure a student’s self-determination capacity and opportunity. The AIR Self-Determination Scale is available through the University of Oklahoma Zarrow Center. Use results to set meaningful IEP goals.

  • Parent Communication Letter

    A ready-to-send letter introducing families to student-led IEP practices and inviting their partnership in the process.

  • Student Progress Rubric

    A structured tool for tracking and sharing student growth in self-determination and IEP participation skills over time.

  • TAGG Lesson Plans

    Research-based, standards-aligned lesson plans tied to the Transition Assessment and Goal Generator (TAGG). Free and ready to implement immediately in your classroom without additional preparation.

  • Literature Connection Guide

    A guide for using books, stories, and media as vehicles for self-determination skill building — including the Tying the Knot resource series featured in Module 5.

  • Meeting Observation Form

    A structured form for observing and reflecting on student participation during IEP meetings. Use it to set goals, document progress, and celebrate growth over time.

Inspiration and Motivation

Success Stories

  • Marcus’s Journey

    Follow Marcus, the student case study from Module 2, as he grows into a confident IEP leader. Available in the Module 2 materials, this case study shows what growth looks like in practice.

  • Garrett Shows: I’m in Charge

    A documentary short by filmmaker Dan Habib following Garrett, a high school student with intellectual disabilities who leads his own IEP meeting. A powerful, real-world example of what student involvement looks like.

  • Gather Educator Voices

    Reach out to mentors, fellow teachers, and networking contacts who have tried student-led IEP practices. Ask what worked, what surprised them, and what they would tell someone starting out — their real voices are some of the most motivating input you can collect.

  • Invite Family Perspectives

    Engage the families you work with and ask for their input and perspective. Hearing how student involvement looks from the home side builds partnership and helps you understand and address what families experience.

Troubleshooting Guide — Common Challenges and Solutions

Challenge: Low student confidence
Solution: Start smaller and celebrate more. Focus on one strength before introducing a challenge. Small wins build the momentum students need to take bigger steps.

Challenge: Time constraints
Solution: Embed practices in existing activities rather than adding new ones. Bell ringers, morning meetings, and check-ins can all carry self-determination practice without adding time.

Challenge: Technology issues
Solution: Always have a low-tech backup ready. Printed scripts, handwritten notes, and verbal sharing work just as well as digital tools when technology fails.

Challenge: Parent resistance
Solution: Share research on student outcomes and start gradually. Letting parents observe before asking them to step back often makes all the difference.

Challenge: Colleague skepticism
Solution: Invite observation. Seeing one student lead their own IEP meeting is often more persuasive than any research summary or data chart.
