SDLMI Phase 1 Goal-Setting Practice

Experience the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction

Activity Instructions

The best way to understand how SDLMI works is to USE it yourself. You'll be able to better explain it to students, better facilitate it in your classroom, and better understand the reflection process if you've experienced it.

Your task: Work through Phase 1 of SDLMI with a personal or professional goal. Think about something YOU want to learn or improve.

Why this matters: When you go through this process yourself, you'll understand the thoughtful self-reflection SDLMI requires. You'll see where students might get stuck and what kinds of support they'll need.

Note: Your responses are automatically saved as you type.

Step 1: Identify Your Goal

Think about something you want to learn or improve professionally. This could be:

  • A teaching strategy you want to implement (e.g., "Involve students more in IEP meetings")
  • A classroom management technique (e.g., "Improve my transition time between activities")
  • A new technology skill (e.g., "Learn to use Google Classroom more effectively")
  • A student engagement strategy (e.g., "Increase student participation during lessons")
  • A professional development goal (e.g., "Better understand co-teaching models")

Step 2: SDLMI Phase 1 - Set a Goal

Phase 1 of SDLMI has you answer three critical questions. Take your time and be specific with your responses.

Question 1: What do I want to learn or improve?

Be as specific as possible. Instead of "be better at classroom management," try "reduce transition time between activities from 5 minutes to 2 minutes."

Example: "I want to learn how to help my students actively participate in their IEP meetings instead of just sitting quietly while adults talk about them."
0 characters

Question 2: What do I know about it now?

Assess your current level honestly. What CAN you already do? What do you understand? What's still confusing or unclear?

Example: "I know that student involvement is important and legally required. I know there are different activities like one-pagers and SPIN statements. But I'm not sure how to actually implement this in my packed IEP schedule, and I don't know which activities to start with for my specific students."
0 characters

Question 3: What must change for me to learn what I don't know?

Think about the GAP between where you are now and where you want to be. What's missing? What needs to happen? Be specific about actions, resources, or support you need.

Example: "I need to choose 2-3 specific activities to try this semester instead of trying to do everything at once. I need to set aside 15 minutes before each IEP meeting to prepare the student. I need to communicate with parents ahead of time so they understand what their child will be doing. I need my administrator's support to slightly modify our IEP meeting format to allow student participation time."
