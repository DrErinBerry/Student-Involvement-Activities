Parent Communication Letter

Student Involvement in the IEP — Oklahoma State Department of Education

This is a ready-to-send letter that introduces families to student-led IEP practices and invites them to partner in the process. It is written to work either as a start-of-year introduction or as a note before an upcoming IEP meeting — just fill in the blanks and adjust the wording to fit your family and student.

How to use this letter
  • Fill in the blanks: date, family name, student name, your name, and your contact information.
  • The bracketed lines in the middle let you tailor the letter for the start of the year or for an upcoming meeting — keep the one that fits and delete the other.
  • Use the Print / Save as PDF button to send it home on paper, or copy the text into an email.


(Date)

Dear ,

I am writing to share something I am excited about for this year. In our classroom, we are helping students take an active role in their own Individualized Education Program (IEP) — the plan that guides their learning and support at school.

You may be used to IEP meetings where the adults do most of the talking. We are working toward something a little different: helping students understand their own strengths, share what helps them learn, and take part in their meetings in a way that feels right for them. Research consistently shows that when students are involved in their own IEPs, they become more confident, more motivated, and better prepared for life after high school. This is a skill that grows over time, and every small step counts.

I want to be clear about what this means for your role: your voice and partnership matter as much as ever. Student involvement does not replace you — it works best alongside you. You know your child in ways no one else does, and the team relies on your perspective. What changes is that your child gradually takes on a more active part, at whatever pace fits them, with all of us supporting them.

[Start of year:] As we begin the year, I wanted you to know about this approach so we can build it together. Over the coming months, your child may practice naming their strengths, setting small goals, or preparing a few things to share at their meeting.

[Before a meeting:] As we prepare for ’s upcoming IEP meeting on , I would love your help getting them ready. We have been practicing at school, and a little encouragement at home goes a long way.

Here are a few simple ways you can partner with us:

• Talk with your child about what they are good at and what they hope for their future.
• Let them know their voice matters and that it is okay to share their thoughts at the meeting.
• Reach out to me with any questions, ideas, or things you would like the team to know.

I would welcome the chance to talk more about this with you — by phone, by email, or in person, whatever is easiest. Please feel free to contact me at .

Thank you for partnering with me to help grow into a confident self-advocate. I am looking forward to what this year holds.

Warmly,
