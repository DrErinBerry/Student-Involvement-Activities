Student-Led IEP Meeting Script

Student Involvement in the IEP — Oklahoma State Department of Education

This is a fill-in-the-blank script a student can use to help lead their own IEP meeting. The student fills in the blanks ahead of time and practices reading it. It is written so a student can lead the opening of the meeting and then hand off to a teacher or facilitator — or, when they are ready, lead the meeting from start to finish.

How to use this script
  • Fill in the blanks with the student before the meeting, and let them practice reading it aloud.
  • The italic notes in gray are coaching tips for the student or adult helper — they are not read aloud.
  • The student can lead as much or as little as they choose. Honor their preference to lead all, part, or none of the meeting.
  • Steps are labeled so you can stop at the handoff point or keep going to run the whole meeting.

Welcome Student leads

“Hi, and thank you for coming to my meeting. My name is . I am years old and I am in the grade.”

Coaching tip: A simple, confident welcome sets the tone. Practice making eye contact and speaking slowly.

My Hopes for the Future Student leads

“After I finish high school, I want to .”

Coaching tip: This can be about college or technical school, a job or career, where they want to live, or what they want to learn. Even a broad idea is a great start.

How Last Year Went Student leads

“Now I want to share how I did on my goals last year. .”

Coaching tip: Review the prior IEP goals together beforehand. The student can name one or two goals and say whether they met them, are getting close, or want to keep working on them.

My Strengths Student leads

“Here are some of my strengths: .”

Coaching tip: Examples to prompt with — hard worker, good listener, friendly, creative, organized, helps others, works well in a group or on my own.

“I would also like my family and teachers to share what they think my strengths are.”

Pause here: Invite family members and teachers to add the strengths they see. Hearing others name their strengths is often a highlight for the student.

What Helps Me Learn Student leads

“The things that help me learn best are .”

Coaching tip: Think about accommodations that worked — extra time, a quiet space, notes ahead of time, working in small groups, using a computer, checklists, or breaks.

“Some things that were hard for me last year were .”

Coaching tip: Framing challenges honestly helps the team plan supports. Keep it matter-of-fact and paired with what would help.

Things I Enjoy Student leads

“When I am not in school, I like to .”

“I have a job right now. .”

Coaching tip: List hobbies, clubs, sports, volunteering, or faith-community activities. If they have a job, they can say what it is and whether it connects to their future goals.

Classes That Match My Goals Student leads

“To reach my goals after high school, the classes I would like to take are .”

Coaching tip: Connect course choices to the future goal named earlier. The case manager can help match courses to the postsecondary goal.

Handing Off Handoff point

“Thank you for helping me share what I want for my future and what works for me in school. For the rest of the meeting, I would like us to plan next year and the supports that will help me reach my goals.”

This is the natural stopping point for a student who is leading the opening. The student can now hand the meeting to the case manager or facilitator — or keep going using the steps below if they are ready to lead the whole meeting.

When the Student Is Ready to Lead the Whole Meeting

A student who has led the opening a few times may want to lead the rest, too. When they are ready, they can continue with these steps instead of handing off. The adult facilitator stays close to support and to make sure every legal requirement is still met.

  1. Review proposed goals: “Let’s look at the goals we are thinking about for this year. Here is what I think I need to work on: .”
  2. Talk through accommodations and supports: “These are the supports I want to keep, and these are ones I want to try.”
  3. Invite the team: “What does everyone else think? Is there anything I am missing?”
  4. Agree on the plan: “Let’s make sure we all agree on my goals and supports for this year.”
  5. Close the meeting: “Thank you all for coming and for helping me plan. This is my plan, and I am glad I got to lead my meeting.”
Facilitator reminder: Even when the student leads the whole meeting, the adult team is responsible for ensuring all required components and procedural safeguards are addressed. The student leads the conversation; the team still owns the legal process.

This script was created for the Oklahoma State Department of Education Student Involvement in the IEP professional learning series as an original, freely usable classroom resource.
